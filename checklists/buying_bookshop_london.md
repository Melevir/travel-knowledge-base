# Sanity Check: Buying an Existing Bookshop in London

Чеклист из 20 пунктов для проверки перед покупкой действующего книжного магазина в Лондоне.

---

## 1. Verify the Financials Against Third-Party Records

- [ ] Request 3-5 years of P&L, balance sheets, **and** tax returns (CT600 or SA100)
- [ ] Cross-reference against **bank statements** and **VAT returns** (filed with HMRC, harder to fabricate)
- [ ] Confirm total bank deposits over 12 months broadly match reported revenue
- [ ] Check EPOS/till data exports for daily transaction counts and average values

> **Why it matters:** Bank statements are the single most reliable verification document. A discrepancy between reported revenue and bank deposits is the biggest red flag.

---

## 2. Understand the True Net Profit (SDE)

- [ ] Calculate Seller's Discretionary Earnings: net profit + owner's salary + personal expenses run through the business
- [ ] Subtract a realistic manager salary (£28-35K in London)
- [ ] Determine your real annual return after a fair wage

> **Benchmarks:**
> - Typical London indie bookshop net margin: **1-5%**
> - Typical annual turnover: **£150K-£500K** (most cluster at £200-350K)
> - Typical owner's effective salary: **£18K-£35K**
> - If SDE is below £30K, the business is a job you're buying, not an investment

---

## 3. Audit the Lease

- [ ] **Remaining term** — under 5 years is risky; 10+ years with security of tenure (Landlord and Tenant Act 1954) is ideal
- [ ] **Rent vs. market** — if below market, the lease itself has assignment value (£30-100K+)
- [ ] **Rent review clauses** — upward-only reviews are common and can destroy margins
- [ ] **Break clauses** — can the landlord terminate early?
- [ ] **Alienation provisions** — can you assign the lease? Landlord consent required under the Landlord and Tenant (Covenants) Act 1995; outgoing tenant typically provides an Authorised Guarantee Agreement (AGA)
- [ ] **Dilapidations liability** — obligation to restore premises at lease end, can cost thousands
- [ ] **Keep-open clauses** — some London leases require continuous trading, blocking temporary closure for refurbishment
- [ ] **Inside or outside the 1954 Act** — if outside (s.38A), no right to renew at lease end

> **This is often the most valuable (or dangerous) asset in the deal.**

---

## 4. Check the Rent-to-Revenue Ratio

- [ ] Calculate annual rent as a percentage of annual revenue

| Ratio | Assessment |
|-------|------------|
| Below 15% | Healthy |
| 15-20% | Manageable |
| 20-25% | Risky |
| Above 25% | **Dealbreaker** |

> **Reference rents** (800 sq ft shop):
> - Prime West End: £120K-£320K/year — unaffordable for indie bookshops
> - Secondary central (Bloomsbury, Marylebone): £40K-£72K
> - Inner suburbs (Hackney, Brixton, Peckham): £24K-£44K
> - Outer boroughs (Dulwich, Muswell Hill, Crystal Palace): £14K-£28K

---

## 5. Evaluate Business Rates and Available Relief

- [ ] Look up the property's Rateable Value (RV) with the Valuation Office Agency
- [ ] Determine applicable relief

| RV | Relief |
|----|--------|
| ≤ £12,000 | 100% Small Business Rate Relief (zero rates) |
| £12,001-£15,000 | Tapered relief |
| > £15,000 | Full rates (multiplier ~55.5p per pound of RV) |

- [ ] Check if Retail, Hospitality and Leisure relief applies (75% relief, capped at £110K/business — subject to annual renewal)
- [ ] Factor in the **April 2026 revaluation** — all rateable values will reset

> Many successful London indie bookshops deliberately choose premises qualifying for SBRR.

---

## 6. Assess Stock Quality and Valuation

- [ ] Conduct a **full physical stock count** with barcode scanning against the EPOS system
- [ ] Value stock at cost, discounted by age:

| Age | Value |
|-----|-------|
| 0-6 months | 100% of cost |
| 6-12 months | 75-80% of cost |
| 12-24 months | 50% of cost |
| 24+ months | 25% of cost or zero |

- [ ] Verify how much stock is **sale-or-return** (belongs to the publisher — must NOT be in the valuation)
- [ ] Negotiate stock separately from goodwill — buyers typically pay **50-75% of cost**

> Typical small bookshop stock at cost: **£15K-£50K**.

---

## 7. Check Stock Turnover Rate

- [ ] Calculate: annual COGS / average stock value

| Turnover | Assessment |
|----------|------------|
| Below 2x/year | Poor — overstocking or dying customer base |
| 3-4x/year | Healthy |
| 5x+/year | Excellent |

- [ ] Check revenue per square foot (London indie benchmark: **£150-£300/sq ft/year**)
- [ ] Check average transaction value (book-only: £12-18; with cafe/gifts: £15-25)

---

## 8. Evaluate Revenue Diversification

- [ ] Break down revenue by source:

| Source | Target % | Gross Margin |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Books | ≤70-75% | 38-43% |
| Cafe/coffee | 15-30% | 65-75% |
| Gifts/stationery/cards | 10-20% | 50-60% |
| Events (tickets + associated sales) | 2-8% | Varies |
| Online (own site + Bookshop.org) | 10-25% | 20-30% (postage eats margin) |

- [ ] Calculate blended gross margin — target **45-48%** (vs. 40% books-only)

> A books-only shop with no diversification is high-risk.

---

## 9. Understand the Competitive Landscape

- [ ] Map every book-selling competitor within a 15-minute walk
- [ ] Assess specific threats:

| Competitor | Threat Level | Why |
|------------|-------------|-----|
| **Waterstones** nearby | High | Scale, better publisher terms, similar curation post-Daunt |
| **Daunt Books** nearby | Medium-High | Competes for affluent literary customer |
| **Foyles** | Low (destination) | Less threat to neighbourhood shops |
| **Amazon** | Indirect | You compete on curation, community, experience |
| **Supermarkets/WHSmith** | Low | Bestsellers only, no backlist |

- [ ] Identify the shop's clear identity: specialism, community hub, cafe hybrid, neighbourhood personality
- [ ] Ask: if this shop closed, would the neighbourhood notice?

---

## 10. Assess Owner Dependency (Personal vs. Enterprise Goodwill)

- [ ] Does the shop have its own social media following, or is it the owner's personal account?
- [ ] Are events tied to the owner's personal network?
- [ ] Do publisher reps and author contacts know the **shop**, or the **owner**?
- [ ] Would regular customers return if the owner left?

> **High personal goodwill = lower valuation.** Consider an **earnout** structure (part of price paid over 1-2 years, contingent on revenue) to manage this risk.

---

## 11. Scrutinize Employee Obligations Under TUPE

- [ ] Confirm TUPE 2006 applies (it almost always does for a going-concern bookshop sale)
- [ ] Review Employee Liability Information (seller must provide at least 28 days before transfer):
  - Identity and age of all employees
  - Employment particulars (s.1 ERA 1996 statements)
  - Disciplinary/grievance actions in the past 2 years
  - Any pending claims or grievances
  - Applicable collective agreements
- [ ] Check accrued but untaken holiday pay
- [ ] Verify auto-enrolment pension obligations
- [ ] Check actual vs. contracted hours

> **All employees transfer automatically** on existing terms. You cannot cherry-pick. Dismissal connected to the transfer is automatically unfair unless there's an ETO reason. Failure to consult: up to **13 weeks' pay per employee**.

---

## 12. Investigate Hidden Liabilities

- [ ] Outstanding debts (loans, overdrafts, COVID bounce-back loans, HP agreements on equipment)
- [ ] Tax arrears (PAYE, VAT, Corporation Tax, business rates)
- [ ] Supplier debts and credit terms — paying Gardners at 90 days on 30-day terms = cash distress
- [ ] Pending or threatened litigation
- [ ] Any "time to pay" arrangements with HMRC
- [ ] Dilapidations exposure under the lease

> **Structure as an asset purchase** (not share purchase) to avoid inheriting unknown liabilities. In a share purchase, you inherit everything — known and unknown.

---

## 13. Calculate Total Acquisition Cost

| Item | Typical Range |
|------|---------------|
| Business purchase (goodwill + fixtures) | £30,000-£150,000 |
| Stock (negotiated separately) | £15,000-£40,000 |
| Solicitor fees | £2,000-£5,000 |
| Accountant / due diligence | £1,500-£4,000 |
| Surveyor (lease review) | £500-£1,500 |
| SDLT on lease assignment | Varies |
| Landlord's legal costs (your obligation) | £1,000-£3,000 |
| Working capital (3-6 months) | £25,000-£70,000 |
| **Realistic total** | **£80,000-£280,000** |

- [ ] Confirm you have capital for the full cost, not just the asking price

> If you only have the asking price in cash, you cannot afford the business.

---

## 14. Validate the Asking Price Against Multiples

- [ ] Calculate price as a multiple of SDE:

| Multiple | Interpretation |
|----------|---------------|
| 1.0-1.5x SDE | Struggling or owner-dependent |
| 1.5-2.5x SDE | Fair range |
| 2.5-3.0x SDE | Well-established, transferable goodwill |
| 4x+ SDE | **Overpriced** |

- [ ] Cross-check: **0.15-0.35x annual revenue**
- [ ] Expect to negotiate to **70-90% of asking price**
- [ ] Quantify the below-market lease value separately — it may be the most valuable asset

---

## 15. Review Seasonal Cash Flow

- [ ] Confirm what % of revenue comes from Christmas (Nov-Dec) — typical: **25-40%**
- [ ] Determine if the business makes money in Jan-Sep or just survives on Christmas
- [ ] Check when Christmas stock must be ordered and paid for (typically Sep-Oct)
- [ ] Identify the worst months (usually Jan-Feb and Jul-Aug)
- [ ] Assess: can the business survive 3 consecutive poor months?

> If the business is only profitable in Q4, it's structurally fragile.

---

## 16. Check Licences, Registrations, and Compliance

- [ ] VAT registration (mandatory above £90K turnover; books are zero-rated but gifts/cafe are not)
- [ ] Employers' Liability Insurance (compulsory, min £5M cover)
- [ ] ICO / data protection registration (if holding customer data, mailing lists, CCTV)
- [ ] Fire risk assessment (Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005)
- [ ] Food hygiene registration if there's a cafe (28 days notice to local authority)
- [ ] Premises Licence if you plan alcohol at events (Licensing Act 2003)
- [ ] Planning use class — retail is Class E; confirm the premises have this use
- [ ] Sunday trading — shops over 280 sq m restricted to 6 hours on Sundays

---

## 17. Assess Online and Digital Infrastructure

- [ ] **Domain name** — does the business own it? Included in the sale?
- [ ] **Social media accounts** — transferable, or tied to the owner's personal profiles?
- [ ] **Bookshop.org** affiliate account status and earnings history
- [ ] **EPOS system** — owned or leased? What data does it hold? Can it transfer?
- [ ] **Customer mailing list** — size, engagement rates, GDPR consent status
- [ ] **Website platform** (Shopify, etc.) — who owns the account?
- [ ] **Google Business Profile** — reviews, rating, SEO ranking

> A shop with 5,000 engaged newsletter subscribers and 10K Instagram followers has real digital goodwill. A shop with nothing is starting from zero.

---

## 18. Talk to Neighbours, Suppliers, and the Booksellers Association

- [ ] **Neighbouring businesses**: footfall trends? Planned developments? Road works?
- [ ] **Key suppliers** (Gardners, Bertrams, major publishers): payment history? Credit concerns?
- [ ] **Booksellers Association**: membership status, benchmark data, any known issues
- [ ] **Local council**: planned changes to the area (construction, BIDs, transport changes)?
- [ ] **Customers**: visit the shop multiple times at different hours/days — how busy is it really?

> Due diligence is not just spreadsheets.

---

## 19. Negotiate a Transition Period and Non-Compete

- [ ] **Seller handover period**: 2-4 weeks minimum (ideally paid) — introductions to publishers, reps, key customers, operational rhythm
- [ ] **Non-compete clause**: seller must not open a competing bookshop within 3-5 miles for 2-3 years (must be in the sale agreement — not automatic)
- [ ] **Supplier introductions**: confirm direct publisher accounts can transfer; some publishers require new credit applications
- [ ] **Earnout option**: if goodwill is disputed, offer 50-70% upfront + remainder over 12-18 months contingent on revenue targets

---

## 20. Stress-Test Your Assumptions

- [ ] Build a financial model with three scenarios:

| Scenario | Assumptions |
|----------|-------------|
| **Base case** | Revenue = last 3-year average, costs as stated |
| **Down case (-20%)** | Revenue drops 20% post-transition (owner goodwill loss, disruption), rent rises at next review |
| **Disaster case (-35%)** | Revenue drops 35%, supplier terms change, energy/wage costs rise 10% |

- [ ] Confirm the business survives the **down case for 12 months** with your available working capital
- [ ] Confirm the business survives the **disaster case for 6 months**
- [ ] If not — you need a bigger cash reserve or a lower purchase price

## Bottom Line

The median London indie bookshop is a passion project with thin margins, not a wealth-building investment. The economics work when:

1. Rent is low (ideally SBRR-qualifying)
2. Revenue is diversified beyond books
3. The community is loyal
4. You have enough working capital to survive the first year of transition

This checklist separates genuine viable businesses from lifestyle businesses that only work because the current owner underpays themselves.
