## CRITICAL FRAMING NOTE

**The checklist `buying_bookshop_london.md` is built around an *indie bookshop* business model.** The target supplied here is **NOT a bookshop**. AntikBar is a **vintage poster gallery and auction house** in Chelsea. The SIC code (47791 — "Retail sale of antiques including antique books in stores") covers both, but the operational, margin, stock-turn, supplier and rent-to-revenue economics of a vintage poster gallery differ materially from those of a books retailer:

| Bookshop economics | Vintage poster gallery economics |
|---|---|
| Books gross margin 38–43% (RPM/discount-driven) | Posters: 50–70%+ (wholesale lots, auction acquisitions) |
| Stock turn target 3–4x/year | Stock turn typically <1x/year (slow-moving, high unit value) |
| Stock at cost £15K–£50K | Stock at cost £600K–£950K (per filed accounts) |
| Avg transaction £12–£25 | Avg transaction £100–£10,000 (per AntikBar's own pricing notes) |
| Books are zero-rated for VAT | Vintage posters/art are standard-rated (20%) — partial second-hand margin scheme may apply |
| Sale-or-return supply via Gardners/Bertrams | No equivalent — all stock owned outright |
| Christmas-driven seasonality | Auction-cycle and art-fair-driven seasonality |

The 20-point checklist still works as a structural framework, but several specific benchmarks in it (rent ratio band, margin band, stock-turn band, sale-or-return question, publisher/distributor questions, Booksellers Association reference) must be replaced or reinterpreted for art-trade norms. **I have flagged this throughout. Do not import the bookshop benchmarks unmodified.**

A second framing point: **no public sale listing was found** for AntikBar. There is no entry on `uk.businessesforsale.com`, no Daltons listing, no Rightbiz listing, no press announcement of sale, retirement or closure, and no compulsory strike-off / Gazette / insolvency record. As of 2026-05-04 the company is filing accounts and confirmation statements normally, the auctions calendar shows scheduled events into 2026, and the founder/owner is the active chair of IVPDA (the international trade body) for 2025. **If a sale is being negotiated, it is private/off-market.** All "asking price" / "stock for sale" information needs to be requested directly — none of it is in the public domain.

### 13. Calculate Total Acquisition Cost

**No asking price has been published.** Without a price, the figure cannot be modelled. The asset/share question, however, is critical here:

**Asset purchase view:**
- Tangible fixed assets: £1,231 NBV (residual)
- Stock at cost: £850,632 (the dominant asset)
- Goodwill / brand / domain: subjective
- Lease assignment: must be negotiated (assignment fees, AGA, landlord legal costs ~£1–3K)
- Cash: not transferred in an asset deal (stays with seller)

**Share purchase view:**
- The buyer steps into the company at a net asset value of **£972,430** (FY2024). This is the *floor* below which a share-deal price would not be rational from the seller's side — it's their book equity.
- The buyer would also inherit the £127,526 cash, which offsets some of the price.
- Buyer inherits: VAT registration, auction-platform accounts (if held in company name), IVPDA membership (if it transfers), trade history, customer database
- Buyer inherits ALL liabilities — known and unknown. Given 10+ years of trading, robust warranties + indemnities are essential.

**Critical structuring questions to ask:**
- [ ] Is the seller offering an asset sale or a share sale?
- [ ] What is the asking price, and what does it include / exclude?

### 14. Validate the Asking Price Against Multiples

| Metric | Source | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Price ÷ SDE | Common SME benchmark | Test against 1.5–3x range |
| Price ÷ EBITDA | More appropriate for owner-managed businesses with multiple add-backs | Test against 3–5x for art trade |
