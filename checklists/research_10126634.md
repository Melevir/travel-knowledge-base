**What this means for a prospective buyer:**

| If seller proposes... | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Buy the shares of SKOOB BOOKS LIMITED" | You would acquire an empty shell — name protection only, no lease, no stock, no goodwill, no VAT registration, no customers. The trading business does **not** sit inside the company. |

Several standard checklist items (CT600 corporation tax returns, company P&L history, director's remuneration add-backs, share transfer mechanics) **do not apply** as normally stated, because the company has no trading P&L. ## Companies House: the dormant-company balance sheet

Because the company has only ever filed dormant accounts, the balance sheet contains no trading activity. A dormant company's filed accounts typically show only **called-up share capital** and an equal balancing figure. There is therefore **no P&L, no turnover, no stock, no cash, no creditors, and no liabilities visible in Companies House filings** for 10126634 — not because these don't exist in real life, but because they sit with the sole trader and never enter the limited company.

## Bottom line

This is a well-established, distinctive secondhand bookshop with a strong central-London location and a meaningful 23-year online presence, but the diligence path here differs from a typical incorporated indie-bookshop acquisition because:

- The limited company is a dormant shell — **the trading business is a sole trader**, and the legal, tax, and financial due diligence have to match that form
- The business's value is concentrated in **three transferable-with-difficulty assets** (the Brunswick lease, the AbeBooks rating, and the owner's curation/sourcing network), each of which requires active counterparty consent or a credible handover plan to survive the sale
- The Didcot warehouse is a material unknown — if its ownership is personal, the "million-book" stock may be partially outside the deal
- There is currently no asking price, revenue, or profit figure in the public domain, so the buyer is entirely dependent on information disclosed by the seller — making bank statement and VAT-return verification especially important

Proceed only after Section A (deal structure) and Section B (financials) are fully answered in writing, and only then commission professional advisers.
